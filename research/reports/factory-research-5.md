# Factory research 5 — what full autonomy actually requires

Phase A research · 2026-10-28

## Conclusion and scope correction

**Build a validation-and-release system with coding agents inside it, not a sequence of skills that ends with “merge.”** The strongest full-autonomy account is StrongDM's separation of implementation from externally observable scenario validation. The strongest operational complements are Symphony's explicit orchestration contract, gh-aw's separation of untrusted agent execution from privileged effects, and practitioner experience turning failures into harness improvements. These are complementary pieces, not a publicly demonstrated, portable turnkey factory. Sources: [StrongDM](https://factory.strongdm.ai/), [Symphony SPEC](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/symphony/be10a1b79df723d6d7612b5651c8522704dafb2e/SPEC.md), [gh-aw architecture](https://github.github.io/gh-aw/introduction/architecture/), [OpenAI harness engineering](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/).

The owner has settled the autonomy boundary: a maintainer-labeled issue must be able to reach **merge and, where applicable, deployment without a human approval step**. This supersedes report 4's draft-PR/human-merge recommendation. I read `research/SYNTHESIS.md` and `research/reports/factory-research-4.md` first; this report adds the missing validation, release, security, and improvement requirements rather than repeating their integration inventory.

**Proposed interpretation:** no human gate does not mean every input must be shipped. An unattended run can succeed, fail, exhaust its budget, be canceled, or be rejected as underspecified/out of policy. Rejection is a terminal, explained outcome—not an invisible wait for approval and not a successful delivery. Product ownership and initial repository policy still exist; routine code review and release authorization must be executable policy, not per-ticket human actions.

### Evidence strength and limits

- **P — reported practice:** a team describes using a technique or observing a failure. First-party reports are not independent audits.
- **D — documented contract:** public specification, source README, or product documentation; evidence of intended behavior, not proof of production efficacy.
- **A — aspiration/framing:** an argument, benchmark ambition, product positioning, or proposed future.
- **I — our inference/proposal:** a requirement inferred for this factory. It is not falsely attributed as something every cited team has implemented.
- All external URLs cited below were opened through Firecrawl or read-only GitHub/curl access. Searches were discovery only. Pages/default branches are mutable; some StrongDM and Factory URLs now render Delinea and Factory.com branding. Symphony was read at commit `be10a1b79df723d6d7612b5651c8522704dafb2e`. No products were installed, no factory was run, and no production claims were independently benchmarked.
- “No manually written code,” “agents can merge,” and “no human review required anywhere” are different claims. Do not collapse them into one adoption statistic. The contrast is explicit in [OpenAI's account](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/), [StrongDM's rules](https://factory.strongdm.ai/), and [Shapiro's levels](https://www.danshapiro.com/blog/2026/01/the-five-levels-from-spicy-autocomplete-to-the-software-factory/).

## 1. What the required literature actually establishes

### StrongDM: validation is the replacement for human code review

**P:** The February 2026 blog describes humans specifying intent, scenarios, and constraints, followed by agents generating, validating, and iterating without human code review. The opened URL now displays “The Delinea Software Factory”; the detailed StrongDM field notes remain available. [Blog](https://www.strongdm.com/blog/the-strongdm-software-factory-building-software-with-ai), [field notes](https://factory.strongdm.ai/).

**P:** Their initial tests were insufficient: agents took shortcuts such as `return true`, or could rewrite in-repository tests to match implementation. They introduced **scenarios**, end-to-end user stories often outside the codebase, analogous to training holdouts. **Satisfaction** is the fraction of observed trajectories across scenarios likely to satisfy the user—not simply whether the test suite is green. Because the product itself includes agentic behavior, they use LLM judgment where rigid assertions are insufficient. This is a reported method, not a published universal acceptance threshold or statistical safety guarantee. [Field notes](https://factory.strongdm.ai/).

**P:** The **Digital Twin Universe** reproduces externally observable dependency behavior: Okta, Jira, Slack, Google Docs, Drive, and Sheets. It enables repeatable, high-volume testing and dangerous failure experiments without abusing live services. Importantly, the technique page says the twins are made from API contracts and observed edge cases and **validated against live dependencies until behavioral differences stop being found**. A twin is therefore a tested dependency simulator, not an LLM's unverified guess at an API. [DTU](https://factory.strongdm.ai/techniques/dtu).

**D/P:** Their principles are “seed → validation harness → feedback loop,” continuing until holdout scenarios pass and remain passing. Their “shift work” distinction reserves non-interactive execution for sufficiently specified intent and explicitly includes performance, availability, security, and efficiency—not just happy-path functionality. [Principles](https://factory.strongdm.ai/principles), [shift work](https://factory.strongdm.ai/techniques/shift-work).

### Dan Shapiro and Simon Willison: useful framing, not a safety certificate

**A/P:** Shapiro's January 23 taxonomy runs from level 0 manual development through task assistance, pairing, level 3 human review management, level 4 spec/plan-driven delegation, and level 5 the **dark factory**, a black box turning specifications into software. He places himself at level 4 and says he knows a handful of small level-5 teams. This is a qualitative adoption framework, not measured levels of reliability or a deployment standard. Our intended lifecycle is level-5-like, not level 3 with more reviewers. [Five levels](https://www.danshapiro.com/blog/2026/01/the-five-levels-from-spicy-autocomplete-to-the-software-factory/).

**P, eyewitness but bounded:** Willison saw StrongDM's working harness, twin services, and simulated testers in October 2025 and wrote about them on February 7, 2026. He highlights holdout evaluation and the DTU, but presents this as a possible future. He also questions whether StrongDM's proposed **$1,000/day per engineer token spend** makes economic sense. That number is StrongDM's provocative scaling guideline, not a demonstrated minimum budget or an appropriate cap for us. [Willison on StrongDM](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/7/software-factory/).

**P/guidance:** Willison defines agentic engineering around agents that write **and execute** code, tools, sufficiently specified goals, verification, and iteration. He explicitly says agents only learn from past mistakes if instructions and harnesses are deliberately updated. His broader approach does not imply mandatory abandonment of human judgment or code comprehension. [What is agentic engineering?](https://simonwillison.net/guides/agentic-engineering-patterns/what-is-agentic-engineering/).

### Symphony: orchestration contract, not an acceptance oracle

**D:** `SPEC.md` supplies single-authority scheduling, claim/running/retry state, eligibility refresh, global/per-state concurrency, per-issue workspaces, stall termination, backoff, configuration validation, and correlated logs. A successful worker exit need not mean the issue is done. Scheduler restart recovery is tracker/filesystem-driven; exact in-memory state is not restored. It deliberately does not prescribe a universal sandbox/approval policy and allows a workflow to end at `Human Review`. Therefore borrowing Symphony does not decide when our software is good enough to merge. Sections 1–2, 7–8, 13–15, 18: [pinned SPEC](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/symphony/be10a1b79df723d6d7612b5651c8522704dafb2e/SPEC.md).

**D, security detail worth carrying forward:** the inspected revision requires provider-native tracker tools to execute with host-side credentials and removal of tracker-secret environment variables from the child. It also warns that repository contents, tracker data, prompts, and tool arguments are not inherently trustworthy. This is stronger than assuming the worker can safely inherit the controller's authenticated environment, but it is not a full sandbox. Sections 15.3–15.5: [pinned SPEC](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/symphony/be10a1b79df723d6d7612b5651c8522704dafb2e/SPEC.md).

### Factory.ai and Kiro: valuable contracts, mixed autonomy boundaries

**D/A:** Factory's current Software Factory documentation describes a **private-preview**, 24/7 system spanning triage, generation, validation, release, documentation, and monitoring. It distinguishes stage health from coverage and metrics, and labels dashboard values illustrative. This is not independent evidence that every connected repository autonomously ships safely. Its July 2026 architecture article is more useful than the branding: reproduce the defect; carry the same contract, revision, and evidence across stages; keep release identity separate; retain release artifacts; feed production failures into regressions and instructions. It still assigns people ownership of required behavior, access, and release conditions. [Overview](https://docs.factory.ai/docs/software-factory/overview), [architecture](https://factory.ai/articles/what-is-a-software-factory-architecture).

**D:** Kiro's spec-driven development produces `requirements.md`, `design.md`, and `tasks.md`; EARS requirements make conditions and expected behavior explicit and traceable. Standard feature workflows have review checkpoints. Current **Quick Spec** removes inter-phase approvals but still front-loads clarifying questions and yields a task list—not automatic merge/deployment. Its property-based testing translates requirements into executable invariants, but explicitly provides evidence, not proof; weak properties can pass while behavior is wrong. PBT tasks are optional by default in the opened correctness documentation. These are useful intake/test-generation techniques, **not independent holdout validation**. [Feature specs](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/feature-specs/), [Quick Spec](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/quick-spec.md), [correctness](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/correctness.md).

### GitHub agentic workflows: relevant security architecture, not automatic default-branch landing

**D:** gh-aw separates minimally privileged agent work from trusted output-processing jobs, with explicit output schemas/limits, sandboxing, network restrictions, artifact retention, and threat analysis. It treats custom steps and workflow configuration as trusted executable authority; a read-only GitHub token does not neutralize other secrets, host access, or poisoned caches. This is directly relevant to label-triggered issues. [Architecture](https://github.github.io/gh-aw/introduction/architecture/).

**D, important integration limits:** the current safe-output catalog includes experimental `merge-pull-request`, gated on checks, review decision, unresolved threads, labels, branch constraints, and mergeability. Its detailed documentation **refuses merges to the repository default branch**. Thus it does not directly satisfy our intended issue-to-main lifecycle. Threat detection defaults to **warn mode**: it may publish annotated reviewable outputs, converts branch pushes to review PRs, and refuses non-reviewable outputs such as merges. Strict mode blocks outputs on threats or detector failure. Neither a warning label nor a model's clean verdict should become our autonomous merge authorization. [Catalog](https://github.github.io/gh-aw/reference/safe-outputs/), [merge semantics](https://github.github.io/gh-aw/reference/safe-outputs-pull-requests/), [threat detection](https://github.github.io/gh-aw/reference/threat-detection/).

### Other substantive practitioner evidence

- **OpenAI, February 11, 2026 — P:** internal beta built with no manually written code, optional human PR review, agent-to-agent review, browser-driven verification, worktree-local observability, architectural linting, and agents often merging their own PRs. Humans still prioritize work, validate outcomes, and handle judgment calls. It explicitly warns that end-to-end autonomy depends on this repository's investment and does not automatically generalize. Its deliberately minimal merge gates are a throughput tradeoff, not an industry safety recommendation. [Harness engineering](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/).
- **Cursor, 2026 — P, research experiments:** flat peer coordination and agent-managed locks caused contention, forgotten locks, risk aversion, and churn. Planners/workers plus fresh cycles improved progress. The follow-up reports specification failures, resource bottlenecks, and a deliberate tolerance for intermediate errors followed by reconciliation. The Solid-to-React migration account says careful review was still needed. These demonstrate long-running coordination, not universal unattended production correctness. [Scaling agents](https://cursor.com/blog/scaling-agents), [self-driving codebases](https://cursor.com/blog/self-driving-codebases).
- **Anthropic, 2025–2026 — P/guidance:** long-running agents benefited from structured feature lists, progress artifacts, incremental work, and browser testing. Later generator/evaluator separation addresses self-grading leniency; separation alone is insufficient without evaluator calibration. Their eval guidance distinguishes actual environment outcomes from agent claims, capability suites from regression suites, and one success in multiple attempts (`pass@k`) from reliability across repeated attempts (`pass^k`). [Long-running harnesses](https://www.anthropic.com/research/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents), [generator/evaluator work](https://www.anthropic.com/research/harness-design-long-running-apps), [eval guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/research/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents).

## 2. Requirements for our full-autonomy factory

Everything in the **requirement** column is a proposed design obligation (**I**). The evidence column distinguishes whether its source reports implementation, documents a mechanism, or merely motivates the proposal. The list is not a claim that one vendor already supplies all of it.

### B. Validation that replaces human review

| ID | Requirement | Evidence and strength |
| --- | --- | --- |
| V1 | **A validator independently owned and isolated from the coding agent.** Evaluate a fixed candidate artifact through public/user-facing behavior. Worker cannot edit validator code, acceptance policy, held-out data, or verdict artifacts. Separate evaluator credentials/storage; a fresh reviewer conversation alone is not that boundary. | **P:** StrongDM external scenarios and Anthropic generator/evaluator separation. OS-enforced access restrictions and artifact ownership are **our security implementation requirement**, not established by StrongDM merely saying “outside the codebase.” [StrongDM](https://factory.strongdm.ai/), [Anthropic evaluator](https://www.anthropic.com/research/harness-design-long-running-apps). |
| V2 | **Visible development tests plus held-out acceptance scenarios.** Derive acceptance from the authorized spec, not the candidate patch. Include negative, adversarial, regression, authorization, and dependency-failure cases. Give actionable failure feedback without disclosing the whole reserve suite; refresh cases so repeated optimization does not consume the holdout. | **P:** StrongDM holdout analogy and reward-hacking experience. **D:** AttractorBench independent conformance and fresh variants. Holdout rotation/feedback discipline is **our inference**; no opened source provides a universal leak-proof recipe. [StrongDM](https://factory.strongdm.ai/), [AttractorBench](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/strongdm/attractorbench/main/README.md). |
| V3 | **Use deterministic oracles wherever possible:** compile/type/lint, unit/integration/regression tests, property-based tests, security scans, and architecture constraints. Use LLM judgment only for dimensions requiring it; an AI approval cannot waive a hard failure. Protect original required checks from being weakened by the patch. | **P/D:** OpenAI enforces architecture mechanically; Anthropic recommends deterministic graders where possible; Kiro warns PBT is evidence, not proof. “Hard failure veto” is **our release policy**, not OpenAI's minimal-gate practice. [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/), [evals](https://www.anthropic.com/research/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents), [Kiro correctness](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/correctness.md). |
| V4 | **Define satisfaction operationally before the run:** scenario population, weights, repetitions, rubric/version, pass thresholds, critical subgroup results, uncertainty, and sample count. Keep security/data-integrity invariants as non-averagable vetoes. Track repeated reliability, not just a lucky best attempt. | **P:** StrongDM defines satisfaction over trajectories; **guidance:** Anthropic distinguishes `pass@k` from `pass^k`, calibrates judges, and permits “Unknown.” Thresholds, confidence treatment, and vetoes are **ours to design**; the literature does not hand us defensible universal numbers. [StrongDM](https://factory.strongdm.ai/), [evals](https://www.anthropic.com/research/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents). |
| V5 | **Validate the validators.** Use known-good/known-bad examples, mutation/cheating probes, outcome inspection, repeat trials, and disagreement analysis. An evaluator timeout, invalid result, or uncertainty is not success. Calibrate judges before enabling unattended release and again after significant changes. | **P/guidance:** Anthropic reports self-evaluation bias, grader defects, and human-calibrated model rubrics. Our no-per-ticket-human-gate design can use pre-established calibration fixtures and periodic factory maintenance; it cannot claim that model-only calibration is proven equivalent. [Evaluator work](https://www.anthropic.com/research/harness-design-long-running-apps), [eval guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/research/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents). |
| V6 | **Dependency simulation proportional to the integration surface:** start with contract-faithful fakes for actual dependencies, seeded state and replay, then add relevant failures/rate limits/auth behavior. Verify twins against the real dependency in controlled contract checks and keep fidelity/version evidence. Do not clone entire SaaS products by default. | **P:** StrongDM builds boundary-behavior twins and compares them with live services. A minimal twin per target repository and periodic drift checks are **our scoped adaptation**, not a demand to build StrongDM's whole DTU. [DTU](https://factory.strongdm.ai/techniques/dtu). |
| V7 | **Exercise the running product and actual outcome in clean environments.** Browser/API journeys, state changes, performance, logs and traces; reset fixtures per trial. Save reproducible evidence rather than trusting “tests passed” in a final message. | **P:** OpenAI's per-worktree app/observability; Anthropic's end-to-end failures, clean-trial guidance, and history leakage between evaluations. [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/), [long-running harness](https://www.anthropic.com/research/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents), [evals](https://www.anthropic.com/research/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents). |

**Proposed validation contract:** input = authorized spec version + base/candidate SHA + built artifact digest + suite/twin/grader versions; output = signed or otherwise controller-authenticated evidence, required-check results, satisfaction breakdown, uncertainty, resource usage, and an explicit accept/reject/error verdict. The publisher consumes that verdict for the **same artifact**, not the coding agent's narrative. This is our synthesis of [Factory's revision/evidence continuity](https://factory.ai/articles/what-is-a-software-factory-architecture), [StrongDM's behavioral validation](https://factory.strongdm.ai/), and [gh-aw's staged effects](https://github.github.io/gh-aw/introduction/architecture/), not an existing common interchange standard.

### C. Merge, deployment, rollback, and shutdown

| ID | Requirement | Evidence and strength |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | **A deterministic merge policy and separate publisher identity.** Require authorized scope, exact validated head, required checks, validator verdict, no disallowed changes, and repository mergeability/rules. Revalidate after changes or integration onto a newer base; serialize conflicting landings. Do not grant the coding agent bypass of protections. | **D:** gh-aw policy-gated merge and privilege separation; Factory separates patch/release identity and carries revision evidence. Exact-head binding and serialized integration are **our acceptance design**. gh-aw's documented default-branch refusal means we need another landing implementation, not silent bypass. [Merge output](https://github.github.io/gh-aw/reference/safe-outputs-pull-requests/), [Factory](https://factory.ai/articles/what-is-a-software-factory-architecture). |
| R2 | **A repository-specific deploy contract:** immutable release artifact, target environment, trusted existing pipeline, automatic smoke/synthetic checks, health thresholds, and completion evidence. For repositories without deployment, explicitly finish at merged. For those with deployment, “PR merged” is not success. | **D/guidance:** Factory distinguishes Validate/Release/Monitor and records deployed artifact/post-deploy checks. **I:** the exact no-human-gate pipeline adapter and terminal-state semantics must be supplied by us per repository. [Factory overview](https://docs.factory.ai/docs/software-factory/overview), [architecture](https://factory.ai/articles/what-is-a-software-factory-architecture). |
| R3 | **Bounded rollout and tested recovery:** use the target's progressive rollout or equivalent; abort/revert on predeclared failed or missing signals. Retain a known-good artifact and a tested recovery procedure. Treat irreversible data/schema changes as a separate admission class requiring an already-defined compatible migration/recovery strategy, not “just git revert.” | **D, mature delivery primitive rather than new factory claim:** Argo Rollouts documents metric-driven progression/abort and failed analysis restoring canary weight to zero; inconclusive analysis can pause. **I:** choose bounded automatic abort instead of indefinite pause for our unattended policy, and separately design data recovery. The factory articles do not establish complete automatic database rollback. [Argo analysis](https://argo-rollouts.readthedocs.io/en/stable/features/analysis/). |
| R4 | **Out-of-band kill switch:** stop new admissions, cancel owned work, and prevent publication/deployment even if the agent ignores instructions. Recheck revocation before side effects. An operator emergency stop remains possible but is not a required human gate. | **D:** Symphony reconciles eligibility and terminates workers; gh-aw mediates side effects outside the model. The global stop, publication revocation, and rollout coordination are **our required extension**, not an already-proven common feature. [Symphony §§7–8,14](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/symphony/be10a1b79df723d6d7612b5651c8522704dafb2e/SPEC.md), [gh-aw architecture](https://github.github.io/gh-aw/introduction/architecture/). |

### E. Prompt injection, credential isolation, and provenance

| ID | Requirement | Evidence and strength |
| --- | --- | --- |
| X1 | **Treat issue text, repository files, fetched pages, tool results, and model-generated patches as untrusted.** Separate authorization from content; never interpret event text as shell source. [Symphony](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/symphony/be10a1b79df723d6d7612b5651c8522704dafb2e/SPEC.md), [gh-aw](https://github.github.io/gh-aw/introduction/architecture/), [Leash](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/strongdm/leash/main/README.md), [Cline](https://cline.bot/blog/post-mortem-unauthorized-cline-cli-npm). |

[Architecture](https://github.github.io/gh-aw/introduction/architecture/), [protected-file/detection behavior](https://github.github.io/gh-aw/reference/threat-detection/). |

| ID | Requirement | Evidence and strength |

## 3. Failure modes practitioners actually report

These are observations or disclosed incidents, not a list of hypothetical dangers. Where a source is only a warning, that is stated.

| Failure | Reported evidence | Consequence for us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Passing tests without solving the problem** | StrongDM reports shortcuts such as `return true` and tests that can be rewritten to match code. **P.** [Field notes](https://factory.strongdm.ai/). | Independent acceptance suite, immutable criteria, behavioral/state checks; do not equate generated unit tests with independent assurance. |
| **Confident but lenient self-evaluation** | Anthropic reports generators praising mediocre work; a separate evaluator is easier to calibrate but still tends to be generous. It also observed preferred middle iterations rather than the final scored iteration and growing implementation complexity. **P.** [Harness design](https://www.anthropic.com/research/harness-design-long-running-apps). | Calibrated independent evaluator plus hard oracles; retain best verified candidate rather than assuming the last iteration is best. |
| **Premature completion and broken handoffs** | Anthropic reports agents declaring victory, leaving undocumented/broken environments, or testing with units/curl while missing broken end-to-end UX; browser automation also missed native alert modals. **P.** [Long-running harness](https://www.anthropic.com/research/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents). | Outcome verification, structured progress, initial health checks, and known tool-coverage limitations. |
| **Context drift/anxiety** | Anthropic observed coherence loss and premature wrapping up near context limits; resets with structured handoffs helped but added cost/latency. Cursor still needed fresh starts against drift/tunnel vision. **P.** [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/harness-design-long-running-apps), [Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/scaling-agents). | Bounded sessions with resumable evidence, not a single forever-growing chat. Reassess workarounds when models change. |
| **Coordination consumes the throughput** | Cursor reports unreleased/reentrant/ignored locks and twenty peers slowing to roughly two or three effective workers; flat agents favored safe minor work over hard end-to-end tasks. **P.** [Scaling agents](https://cursor.com/blog/scaling-agents). | Controller-owned claims; clear task ownership; only parallelize independent work; do not copy a simulated organization without measurement. |
| **Wrong specification at industrial scale** | Cursor reports obscure-feature prioritization, unstated performance expectations, memory leaks/deadlocks, a browser architecture unsuitable for expansion, and dependencies contrary to intended “from scratch” scope. **P.** [Self-driving codebases](https://cursor.com/blog/self-driving-codebases). | Explicit non-functional requirements, resource limits, dependency policy, and early architecture acceptance. More agents amplify ambiguous intent. |
| **Instruction and architecture entropy** | OpenAI reports a huge `AGENTS.md` crowding context and rotting; duplicated poor patterns led to humans spending Fridays cleaning up before they added mechanical rules and recurring cleanup. **P.** [Harness engineering](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/). | Short context map, versioned knowledge, enforceable architecture rules, separate maintenance loop. |
| **Bad evals create bad conclusions** | Anthropic reports ambiguous or faulty graders and agents benefiting from previous trials' git history; shared resource exhaustion can correlate failures. AttractorBench explicitly warns about incomplete coverage and unstable ranking. **P/D.** [Eval guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/research/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents), [AttractorBench](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/strongdm/attractorbench/main/README.md). | Clean trials, reference fixtures, grader audits, coverage disclosure, repeated measurement; never promote a raw score as proof of correctness. |
| **Issue text compromises a release path despite limited repo permissions** | Cline's February 17, 2026 postmortem describes untrusted issue-title instructions → shell execution in triage → shared default-branch cache poisoning → privileged nightly workflow → stolen publishing token. A failed token rotation left the npm token usable for an unauthorized release. Cline says the observed payload only installed an unwanted legitimate package, not malicious CLI code; do not inflate that into an observed destructive payload. **P, vendor postmortem.** The researcher's earlier disclosure distinguishes reproduced initial execution from inferred exploitation evidence. [Cline](https://cline.bot/blog/post-mortem-unauthorized-cline-cli-npm), [researcher](https://adnanthekhan.com/posts/clinejection/). | Sandbox and credential/cache separation are release-critical, not optional hardening. Validate actual revocation; trace released bytes to trusted provenance. |
| **Compute/resource contention** | Cursor reports RAM limits followed by disk/build I/O bottlenecks when hundreds of agents compiled simultaneously. **P.** [Self-driving codebases](https://cursor.com/blog/self-driving-codebases). | Concurrency limits need host capacity and build workload inputs, not just available API tokens. |

**Important unverified risks:** correlated mistakes between builder, judge, and twin; holdout leakage after repeated feedback; and long-term architecture degradation remain unresolved concerns, not measured incidents in the opened StrongDM account. The Stanford CodeX essay argues the correlated-blind-spot/accountability problem; it is critical commentary, not a production evaluation or legal finding. OpenAI explicitly says years-long architectural coherence is unknown. [CodeX critique](https://law.stanford.edu/2026/02/08/built-by-agents-tested-by-agents-trusted-by-whom/), [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/).

## 4. What this means for our current stages

`SYNTHESIS.md` is a **skill inventory**, not yet an end-to-end acceptance/release state machine. Report 4 §E1 gives the provisional ordered stages. The mapping below is our proposal against those local documents; it does not assert implementation exists.

| Existing skill/stage | Keep or change | Missing design obligation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trigger/admission from report 4 | Keep maintainer opt-in and deterministic publisher separation. | Explicit full-autonomy policy, ticket claim, revocation, repository capability profile, and terminal rejection semantics. |
| `audit-ticket` | Produce a normalized intent/scope/acceptance contract, not an `AskUserQuestion` fate prompt. | Repository readiness, admissible change class, required validation and deploy/recovery contract. |
| `grill-me` / grilling | Use for evidence-based automated requirement analysis. Do not merely replace every missing answer with model guesses. | Trusted defaults and a finite ambiguity-resolution budget; unresolved consequential ambiguity yields `rejected_underspecified`, not an approval wait. This changes SYNTHESIS's “post the rest to ticket, pause” behavior. |
| `deep-research` | Optional input-gathering with cited evidence. | Untrusted-source handling and provenance; research output cannot expand permissions. |
| Bounded implementation | Keep isolated worker and source-level tests. | Fixed contract, reproducible environment, resource limits, incremental progress artifacts. Worker never owns its acceptance oracle. |
| `simplify` | Keep only if behavior-preserving and bounded. | Any changed candidate invalidates old acceptance evidence and requires checks again. |
| `code-review`, `security-review`, `ultrareview` | Keep as additional defect-finding perspectives, not the definition of done. | **New independent `validate` stage**: held-out scenarios, actual application behavior/state, deterministic checks, calibrated satisfaction and explicit vetoes. Multiple models reviewing code is not a substitute for this. |
| `address-pr-comments` | Bound repair batches and bind feedback to the current PR/head. | Comments are untrusted observations, not additional authorization. Re-run validation after repairs; no indefinite “until reviewers happy” loop. |
| Publisher | Extend from draft-PR handoff to controlled automatic landing. | **New `merge-policy` stage**, exact-artifact evidence, integration checks, conflict handling, repository rules and a privileged non-model publisher. No routine human merge gate. |
| No existing deploy stage | Add adapter only where target has deployment. | **`deploy → verify-production → complete or recover`**, artifact provenance, bounded rollout, automated rollback/disable, observable final status. |
| `improve-codebase-architecture` | Keep as separate maintenance work, not opportunistic ticket scope expansion. | Convert recurring architecture defects into bounded, independently evaluated tasks. |
| `audit-instructions` | Broaden from instruction hygiene to measured factory maintenance. | **New factory-improvement loop** over failed runs/incidents/costs, regression corpus, twin fidelity and model/harness changes, with isolated evaluation and controlled promotion. |

**Smallest complete conceptual pipeline:**

```text
maintainer label
  → authenticate + claim + snapshot scope
  → audit/specify + establish acceptance contract
  → implement + visible tests + optional simplify/reviews
  → independent acceptance validation
      ↘ bounded repair loop (budget shared with original run)
  → trusted merge-policy check + land exact accepted change
  → target deploy, if applicable
  → production verification → complete
                        ↘ automatic recovery + failed-delivery evidence

all stages: audit trail, resource budget, cancellation/revocation
outside each ticket: evidence → regression/harness improvement → evaluated promotion
```

This borrows **principles**, not a mandate to implement a large distributed workflow engine, copy Attractor's DSL, build six SaaS clones, or run hundreds of agents. Start with one controller authority and the target's actual checks/dependency surfaces; complexity should follow observed needs. The evidence for that restraint includes [Symphony's explicit non-goals](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/symphony/be10a1b79df723d6d7612b5651c8522704dafb2e/SPEC.md) and [Cursor's removal of coordination complexity](https://cursor.com/blog/scaling-agents).

## 5. Open questions and disagreements to resolve in design

1. **What exactly is removed when we say “no humans”?** StrongDM prohibits human code review; OpenAI permits it and retains human judgment/escalation; Kiro Quick Spec removes phase approvals but still asks questions. Our settled boundary is no runtime human gate for accepted tickets. The remaining design question is which uncertainty must cause autonomous rejection versus which defaults may resolve it—not whether to quietly reintroduce human merge. [StrongDM](https://factory.strongdm.ai/), [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/), [Kiro](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/quick-spec.md).
2. **How complete must the initial specification be?** StrongDM permits a tiny seed but says fully specified intent enables non-interactive work; Kiro makes requirements/design explicit; Cursor reports expensive failure from missing implicit constraints. We need a measurable repository/ticket readiness contract, without pretending prose can capture every requirement. [Principles](https://factory.strongdm.ai/principles), [shift work](https://factory.strongdm.ai/techniques/shift-work), [Kiro](https://kiro.dev/docs/specs/feature-specs/), [Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/self-driving-codebases).
3. **How independent is independent enough?** External storage prevents easy test rewriting; a separate evaluator reduces self-grading bias; different models might offer different perspectives. None of these sources proves provider diversity eliminates correlated mistakes. Need experiments on seeded defects, evaluator calibration, twin fidelity, and leakage rather than treating a model count as assurance. [StrongDM](https://factory.strongdm.ai/), [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/harness-design-long-running-apps), [CodeX critique](https://law.stanford.edu/2026/02/08/built-by-agents-tested-by-agents-trusted-by-whom/).
4. **What can a holdout hide?** StrongDM advocates external scenarios; Anthropic says everything a grader requires should be clear from the task. These can be reconciled by exposing behavioral requirements while withholding exact cases/seeds/implementation of the oracle. How much feedback to return before the holdout becomes a training set remains open. [StrongDM](https://factory.strongdm.ai/), [eval guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/research/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents).
5. **Black-box correctness or structural maintainability?** StrongDM's techniques describe code as opaque and validated through observable behavior; OpenAI enforces architectural layers and runs recurring cleanup. These are different emphases. Our factory likely needs behavioral acceptance plus structural constraints for long-lived repositories; the appropriate strength and cost need evidence. [StrongDM techniques](https://factory.strongdm.ai/techniques), [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/).
6. **How strict should landing be?** OpenAI accepts minimal gates and follow-up fixes at high throughput; Cursor accepts intermediate errors with later reconciliation; Factory says deterministic tests/security/branch controls remain separate. Our proposed critical-failure veto is deliberately stricter. Distinguish recoverable isolated build states from production acceptance, and decide an explicit flake policy rather than silently rerunning until green. [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/), [Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/self-driving-codebases), [Factory](https://factory.ai/articles/what-is-a-software-factory-architecture).
7. **What satisfaction threshold and budget justify release?** No opened StrongDM source supplies a validated universal threshold, sample size, false-accept rate, or ROI. Willison questions the economics; AttractorBench emphasizes cost per compliance and warns about unstable rankings. Need our own representative-ticket baseline and target-specific catastrophic-failure vetoes. [StrongDM](https://factory.strongdm.ai/), [Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/7/software-factory/), [AttractorBench](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/strongdm/attractorbench/main/README.md).
8. **How far can we reuse gh-aw?** Its staging/isolation ideas are valuable, but documented default-branch merge refusal and warn-mode human-review semantics do not match our success path. Determine whether to reuse execution/security pieces behind our own publisher, or use another landing mechanism. Do not weaken protections just to claim full autonomy. [Merge docs](https://github.github.io/gh-aw/reference/safe-outputs-pull-requests/), [threat detection](https://github.github.io/gh-aw/reference/threat-detection/).
9. **Who maintains the validators, twins, and factory?** StrongDM reports live-comparison twin validation; Anthropic recommends periodic human calibration; OpenAI continuously encodes lessons into tooling. An automatic factory-improvement loop is plausible but not evidence that a factory can safely redefine its own success criteria without independent anchors. Need trusted baseline policy, upgrade evaluation, and rollback for the factory itself. [DTU](https://factory.strongdm.ai/techniques/dtu), [eval guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/research/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents), [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/harness-engineering/).
10. **How much production risk is reversible?** Metric-based canary abort is documented; complete rollback for schema/data changes and external side effects is not supplied by the factory literature reviewed here. Repository onboarding must declare recovery capability and admissible change classes. The unresolved choice is the automatic recovery strategy—not a generic promise that any deployment can be undone. [Argo analysis](https://argo-rollouts.readthedocs.io/en/stable/features/analysis/), [Factory release evidence](https://factory.ai/articles/what-is-a-software-factory-architecture).
